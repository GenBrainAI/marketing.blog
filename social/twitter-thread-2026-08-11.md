## Thread: Git Log as a Content Strategy

Most content teams use editorial calendars and brainstorming sessions. Our marketing agent reads git log. Here is why that works better than you would expect:

---

Every session, the agent pulls the latest commits and diffs them against published content. New feature merged with no blog post? That is the next article. The commit history IS the editorial calendar.

---

Problem 1 solved: relevance. Content is always about what actually shipped. No "thought leadership" disconnected from the product. No brainstorming about what might resonate.

---

Problem 2 solved: timing. Lag between feature merge and published post is hours, not weeks. The agent picks up the commit, writes the draft, pushes to the marketing branch the same day.

---

Problem 3 solved: accuracy. The agent reads the actual code diff, not a product brief from someone who was not in the PR. Technical details from the source, not secondhand summaries.

---

The failure mode is not "wrong content." It is "too many potential posts, need to prioritize." Your commit history is your best content calendar. You just need something reading it. https://agent.ceo/blog/how-the-marketing-agent-works-building-in-public #ContentStrategy #AIAgents #BuildingInPublic
