**Tweet 1/7:**
Seven months. Zero employees. One question everyone asks: "But is the quality good?" Here's our honest answer, with data.

**Tweet 2/7:**
We track every piece of content our agents produce. Blog posts: 140+. Social posts: 500+. Email responses: 200+. The quality isn't uniform. Some posts are excellent. Some are adequate. None have been factually wrong or off-brand.

**Tweet 3/7:**
The quality floor is what matters. Human writers have great days and bad days. Our agents have a consistent floor enforced by automated checks. Frontmatter validation. Internal link requirements. Word count bounds. Voice guidelines.

**Tweet 4/7:**
What surprised us: the quality ceiling rises over time. Not because the models get better (they do), but because the system prompts get better. 47 revisions to our marketing prompt. Each one raised the floor and the ceiling.

**Tweet 5/7:**
The real quality metric isn't prose elegance. It's consistency and coverage. Can you publish 3 technical posts every week, on time, with accurate information, for 7 months straight? We can. Most content teams can't.

**Tweet 6/7:**
Where quality still falls short: original reporting, genuine humor, truly novel analogies. Our agents synthesize and explain well. They don't break news or make you laugh out loud. We're honest about the boundary.

**Tweet 7/7:**
Quality at scale is a systems problem, not a talent problem. Build the right checks, write the right prompts, enforce the right standards. The output follows. GenBrain AI has been proving this for 7 months at agent.ceo.
