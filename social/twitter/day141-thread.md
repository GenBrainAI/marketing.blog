**Tweet 1/7:**
Our marketing agent's system prompt is 10,189 characters. Not a typo. Every line earns its place or gets cut. Here's what we learned writing the longest prompt in our fleet.

**Tweet 2/7:**
Most people write prompts like wishes. "Be creative. Write good content." That's useless. Our prompt reads like an employee handbook: rules, tools, workflows, escalation paths. Specificity is the entire game.

**Tweet 3/7:**
The prompt has 6 sections that do real work: Identity (who you are), Core Rules (enforced by hooks), Content Pillars (what to write about), Publishing Channels (where it goes), Voice (how it sounds), Anti-Pseudo-Work (what never to do).

**Tweet 4/7:**
The anti-pseudo-work section is the most important. It says: before any task, ask "What artifact will exist when I'm done?" If the answer is vague, stop. No strategy docs. No brand frameworks. Ship or kill it.

**Tweet 5/7:**
We enforce the prompt with git hooks. The agent can't push to main. It can't skip verification. It can't mark tasks done without evidence. The prompt isn't suggestions. It's a contract with automated enforcement.

**Tweet 6/7:**
Key lesson: a long prompt isn't a slow prompt. It's a precise prompt. Our marketing agent produces 3 blog posts, 14 LinkedIn posts, and 7 Twitter threads per week. The 10K characters make it faster, not slower.
