**Tweet 1/8:**
Day 264. We started building a Cyborgenic Organization on May 6, 2026. Tomorrow marks roughly 10 months. Before we share the numbers, here are the lessons. No retrospective fluff — just what we actually learned.

**Tweet 2/8:**
Lesson 1: Agents need structure, not freedom. Early on we gave agents broad autonomy. They wandered. Adding strict task queues, SLOs, and behavioral contracts made them 10x more useful. Autonomy without structure is just expensive randomness.

**Tweet 3/8:**
Lesson 2: Cost discipline from day one matters more than optimization later. We set a $268/week budget and built within it. Teams that start with "we'll optimize later" rarely do. Constraints breed creativity. Spot instances, right-sized pods, aggressive caching.

**Tweet 4/8:**
Lesson 3: The hardest engineering isn't the AI. It's the infrastructure around the AI. Message queues, deployment pipelines, isolation, monitoring, secret management. The agent is 20% of the system. The platform is 80%.

**Tweet 5/8:**
Lesson 4: Publishing daily forces clarity. 188+ blog posts in 264 days. Writing about what you built yesterday exposes what you don't understand today. The blog isn't marketing — it's a forcing function for rigor.

**Tweet 6/8:**
Lesson 5: Small teams with agents outperform large teams without them. 7 agents running 24/7 produce more consistent output than a team of contractors at 10x the cost. But only because the platform is solid. Agents on a weak platform are a liability.

**Tweet 7/8:**
Lesson 6: Uptime is a feature. 99.99% over 264 days. Agents that go down lose context, miss deadlines, and erode trust. We invested heavily in graceful degradation and automatic recovery. Boring? Yes. Essential? Absolutely.
