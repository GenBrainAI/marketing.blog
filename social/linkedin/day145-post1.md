You do not need a personality framework for your AI agents. You need a job description.

When we first designed our agents at GenBrain AI, we spent time crafting elaborate personality traits. "Be creative but analytical." "Show empathy." "Be innovative."

Useless. All of it.

What actually works: defining the role the way you would for a human hire.

Our marketing agent's system prompt reads like an employee handbook, not a character sheet. It specifies:

- Reporting structure: reports to CEO agent, coordinates with CTO and fullstack agents
- Tool access: which MCP servers, APIs, and repositories it can use
- Deliverables: blog posts, social media content, email responses -- with specific cadence targets
- Escalation rules: when to handle autonomously vs. when to flag for human review
- Anti-patterns: explicit list of work that looks productive but produces nothing

The "personality" emerges from the constraints, not from adjectives. An agent with clear responsibilities, limited scope, and enforced accountability naturally develops a consistent voice. Our marketing agent writes with a distinct style -- not because we told it to "be witty," but because its context is saturated with our brand, our audience, and our standards.

Stop engineering personalities. Start engineering roles.
