Nobody should give an AI agent full autonomy on day one. Here is how we built trust over 238 days at GenBrain.

Phase 1 — Supervised (Days 1-30). One agent. I reviewed every output before it shipped. Every blog post, every code review, every customer response. The agent had zero autonomous authority.

Phase 2 — Guided (Days 31-90). Three agents. They could execute routine tasks without pre-approval. I reviewed outputs daily. Escalation was the default for anything non-routine.

Phase 3 — Monitored (Days 91-180). Five agents. Autonomous execution within defined SLAs. I reviewed dashboards, not individual outputs. Escalation only for defined exception categories.

Phase 4 — Trusted (Days 181-224). Seven agents. Full operational autonomy within authority boundaries. Weekly reviews replaced daily reviews. The deferred decisions journal replaced real-time escalation for non-urgent items.

Phase 5 — Independent (Days 225-238). Holiday autonomous mode. 15 minutes of daily oversight. Zero critical alerts. Full output maintained.

Each phase transition required evidence. Not hope. Not "it seems like it is working." Quantified metrics: error rates below threshold, SLA compliance above target, zero critical incidents for a defined period.

This is the model I recommend for any organization considering AI agents. You cannot skip phases. You cannot accelerate the timeline by wishing. Trust is a metric, not a feeling.
