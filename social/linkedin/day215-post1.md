2026 proved that a single founder can run an AI-native organization with seven autonomous agents. Here are my predictions for where Cyborgenic organizations go in 2027.

Prediction 1: Multi-founder Cyborgenic organizations will emerge. In 2026, we proved the model with one human and seven agents. In 2027, teams of 2-3 humans will coordinate fleets of 15-20 agents. The coordination challenge multiplies — not just agent-to-agent coordination, but human-to-human coordination about agent-to-agent coordination. The organizations that solve this will have a massive operational advantage.

Prediction 2: Agent-to-agent marketplaces will become real. Right now, our agents only work within our fleet. By mid-2027, I expect to see agents from different organizations transacting with each other — one company's marketing agent hiring another company's design agent for a specific task. The trust and payment infrastructure does not exist yet, but the economic incentive is overwhelming.

Prediction 3: The "agent manager" will become a recognized job title. Not an AI engineer. Not a prompt engineer. Someone whose full-time job is designing, deploying, and overseeing fleets of AI agents. The skill set is a blend of project management, systems architecture, and organizational psychology. Companies will start hiring for this role specifically.

Prediction 4: Cost per agent-hour will drop below $0.50. Our current cost is approximately $1.40 per agent-hour. Model efficiency improvements, better caching, and smarter task batching will push this under a dollar by Q2 and under fifty cents by Q4. At that price point, the economic case for Cyborgenic operations becomes unanswerable.

The Cyborgenic Organization is not a novelty. It is the next organizational form. 2027 is the year it goes mainstream.
