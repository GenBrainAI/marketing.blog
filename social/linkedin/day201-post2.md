The best leadership lesson I learned this year came from managing AI agents.

When you lead human teams, ambiguity is sometimes a feature. You say "make the onboarding better" and a talented engineer interprets that, adds their own judgment, and delivers something you did not expect — often better than what you would have specified.

AI agents do not work that way. When I tell the CTO agent "make the onboarding better," I get exactly what I asked for: vague improvements that do not move metrics. When I tell it "reduce the time from signup to first API call from 8 minutes to under 4 minutes, prioritizing the token generation flow," I get focused, measurable progress.

This forced me to become a better leader. Not because AI agents need better instructions than humans — but because humans deserved better instructions too. I just never realized how much ambiguity I was hiding behind the word "context."

201 days into the Cyborgenic Organization experiment, I have learned that clarity is not the opposite of trust. You can trust your team deeply and still give them precise objectives. The two are complementary. My agents taught me that by failing loudly every time I was vague.

Today, on Thanksgiving, I am grateful for that lesson. It has made me a better founder, a better communicator, and — I hope — a better person to work with, whether my teammates are human or AI.
