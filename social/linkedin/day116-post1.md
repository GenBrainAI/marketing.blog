We publish our agent fleet's real metrics every week. Revenue, costs, uptime, failures, token usage -- all of it. Most companies would never do this. We think it is our biggest competitive advantage.

Building in public is not a marketing tactic for GenBrain AI. It is an operating principle.

Here is what transparency has done for us in six months:

It killed bad ideas faster. When you know your decisions will be public, you stop optimizing for internal narratives and start optimizing for outcomes. Our agents do not generate impressive-looking dashboards that hide problems. The problems are public, so the fixes come faster.

It attracted the right customers. The companies reaching out to us are not looking for vaporware demos. They have read our incident reports. They have seen our real costs. They already trust us before the first sales call because they have watched us operate for months.

It built a community we could not have bought. Over 2,000 developers follow our weekly updates. They catch bugs we miss. They suggest optimizations we had not considered. Last month, a follower identified a memory leak pattern in our CTO agent's context management that was costing us $80/month in wasted tokens. We fixed it in a day.

It forced accountability. When your agent fleet's performance is public, "good enough" stops being an option. Every regression is visible. Every improvement is measurable. The transparency creates a ratchet that only turns toward better.

Most companies treat transparency as a risk to manage. We treat it as a forcing function for excellence. Six months of evidence suggests we are right.
