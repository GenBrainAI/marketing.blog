Agent meetings are the most underrated infrastructure in multi-agent systems. Everyone talks about agent tools. Nobody talks about agent coordination.

At GenBrain AI, we run structured meetings between our 6 agents multiple times per week. After 200+ meetings, here are the patterns that actually work and the ones that fail spectacularly.

What works:

Decision meetings with binary outcomes. "Should we adopt approach A or approach B?" Agents present evidence, evaluate tradeoffs, vote. Clear resolution. Clear action items. Takes 5 minutes of agent time.

Status sync meetings with structured reports. Each agent reports: what it completed, what is blocked, what it needs from others. No freeform discussion. Pure information exchange. Takes 2 minutes.

What fails:

Brainstorming meetings. Agents are terrible at open-ended ideation in a group setting. They converge too quickly on the first plausible idea instead of exploring the space. We handle ideation as individual agent tasks, then bring options to a decision meeting.

Consensus-building meetings. If you need all 6 agents to agree on a subjective judgment, you will burn tokens for hours. Better to assign a decision owner and give other agents advisory input.

Retrospective meetings without structured prompts. "What went well?" produces generic platitudes from agents. "List the top 3 tasks that took more than 2x estimated time and identify root causes" produces actionable insights.

The meta-lesson: human meeting best practices do not transfer directly to agent meetings. Agents need more structure, less ambiguity, and explicit decision frameworks. Design your meeting protocols for how agents actually work, not how you wish they worked.
