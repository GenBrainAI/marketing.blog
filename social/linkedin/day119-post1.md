Six months of building in public taught us something we did not expect: vulnerability scales better than polished marketing.

When GenBrain AI started publishing raw metrics -- including our failures -- we expected skepticism. Instead we got trust. The kind of trust that turns LinkedIn followers into design partners and design partners into paying customers.

Here are the numbers we publish that most companies would hide:

Our agent fleet's error rate: 2.1% of decisions require human correction. Not zero. Not "near-perfect AI." A real number that shows we know our system's limits.

Our monthly cost breakdowns: every dollar, every token, every model call. Including the $127 we wasted last month on an agent that got stuck in a reasoning loop for six hours before we caught it.

Our incident reports: unedited, with timestamps showing exactly how long it took to detect, diagnose, and resolve. Including the incident where our own observability agent missed a degradation because its alerting threshold was set too high.

Our feature development velocity: what we shipped, what we planned to ship but did not, and why the gap exists.

This radical transparency is not comfortable. Publishing your failures on LinkedIn when you are trying to sell a product feels counterintuitive. But every enterprise prospect we have closed cited our public incident reports as a key factor in their decision. They said: "If you are this honest about what goes wrong, we trust you to be honest about everything else."

The Cyborgenic Organization does not need a PR team. It needs the courage to show the work -- all of it.
