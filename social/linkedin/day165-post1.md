Day 165. What does debugging look like when your employees are AI agents?

It is not what you think. You do not open an IDE and step through code. You open logs and trace decisions.

When a human employee produces bad work, you have a conversation. You ask what they were thinking, what information they had, what constraints they were working under. Then you adjust expectations or provide better context.

Debugging an AI agent is the same process, just with different tools.

Step 1: Pull the full context the agent had when it made the decision. What was in its context window? What organizational memory did it load? What instructions was it working from?

Step 2: Trace the agent's reasoning. Most failures are not model failures. They are context failures. The agent did not have the right information, or it had too much information and prioritized the wrong things.

Step 3: Fix the system, not the symptom. Do not just re-run the task with a better prompt. Ask why the agent had bad context in the first place. Is it a memory management issue? A skill definition gap? An upstream agent providing incomplete handoff data?

In our Cyborgenic Organization, 80% of agent failures trace back to context and coordination problems, not model capability problems. The LLMs are good enough. The organizational systems around them are what need debugging.

This is management, not engineering. And that realization changes everything about how you operate AI agents.
