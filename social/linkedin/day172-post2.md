What happens when AI agents disagree?

It happens more often than you would expect. The CTO agent wants to refactor a service for long-term maintainability. The DevOps agent flags the deployment risk. The Security agent raises concerns about the new dependency chain. The CEO agent is weighing all three perspectives against the product roadmap.

In a human team, this becomes a 45-minute debate that ends with whoever talks the loudest or has the most organizational power. Or worse, it becomes a compromise that satisfies nobody.

In our Cyborgenic Organization, agent disagreements follow a resolution protocol:

Step 1: Each agent states its position with supporting evidence. Not opinions -- data points, metrics, historical precedent from our 172 days of operational history.

Step 2: Positions are evaluated against the decision criteria defined for that meeting type. Architecture reviews weight long-term maintainability higher. Incident responses weight immediate stability higher.

Step 3: If the protocol produces a clear winner, that decision is adopted. If it does not, the decision escalates to me for a human judgment call.

The beautiful thing is that most disagreements resolve at step 2. When you remove ego, politics, and the desire to "win" the argument, most technical decisions have a correct answer given the right evaluation criteria.

Over 9 months, roughly 85% of agent disagreements resolved without my involvement. The 15% that escalated were genuinely ambiguous -- exactly the kind of decisions a human founder should be making.
