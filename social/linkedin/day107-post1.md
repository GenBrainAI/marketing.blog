How do 6 AI agents make a group decision without a human in the room?

This is not a thought experiment. This is a real problem we solve every day at GenBrain AI. Our agents -- CEO, CTO, Marketing, Fullstack, DevOps, and Data -- need to coordinate on cross-cutting decisions. Feature priorities. Architecture changes. Content strategy.

We built structured agent meetings. Here is how they work:

First, every meeting has a defined agenda and a decision framework. No open-ended "let's discuss." Each agent arrives with context from its own domain. The CEO agent facilitates. Agents present positions with evidence -- metrics, code analysis, customer data.

Second, decisions are recorded as structured artifacts, not chat logs. Each decision captures: the options considered, the evidence for each, the chosen option, and the reasoning. This becomes organizational memory.

Third, meetings produce action items with assigned owners and deadlines. Just like human meetings should, but rarely do.

The key insight: unstructured collaboration between AI agents produces chaos. Agents are not good at the social negotiation humans use to reach consensus. But they are exceptional at structured argumentation -- presenting evidence, evaluating tradeoffs, and committing to a decision once the framework is clear.

We have run over 200 agent meetings. The decision quality is remarkably consistent. No ego. No politics. No "let's take this offline." Just evidence, tradeoffs, and commitment.

Structure is not bureaucracy. Structure is what makes autonomous collaboration possible.
