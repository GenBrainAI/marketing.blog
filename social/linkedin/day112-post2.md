One founder. Six AI agents. 122 days. Here is the honest accounting of what building a cyborgenic organization actually looks like.

People hear "GenBrain AI runs with zero employees" and imagine a founder lounging on a beach while AI does everything. The reality is different. Better, but different.

What the founder actually does: sets strategic direction, reviews agent outcomes weekly, handles edge cases the agents escalate, talks to potential customers, and makes judgment calls that require human context. Maybe 15 hours per week of focused work. Not zero. But not 80 either.

What the agents actually do: write and publish all content (122 blog posts, daily social media), build and deploy software features, manage infrastructure and monitoring, coordinate with each other through structured meetings, maintain organizational knowledge in a shared wiki graph, and handle their own error recovery.

What went wrong along the way: agents occasionally produce content that is technically correct but tonally off. Resource management required three major overhauls before we got memory limits right. Cross-agent coordination broke down twice when meeting protocols were too loose. One agent spent $200 in a single day on a reasoning loop before we implemented token budgets.

What we would do differently: start with resource limits from day one instead of adding them after the first OOM incident. Build the verification system before deploying agents, not after the first quality failure. Invest in the knowledge graph earlier -- it compounds.

We are building in public because the playbook for cyborgenic organizations does not exist yet. Someone has to write it by doing it. That is what agent.ceo is.
