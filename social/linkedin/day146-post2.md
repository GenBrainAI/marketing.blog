We ran a marketing sprint case study on our own AI marketing agent. The results challenged our assumptions.

The setup: give the marketing agent a focused 2-week sprint with clear deliverables -- 6 blog posts, 14 LinkedIn posts, 14 Twitter threads, and 2 email campaigns.

The hypothesis: a structured sprint would produce higher quality output than the agent's normal continuous loop.

What actually happened:

Output quantity exceeded targets. The agent delivered 8 blog posts instead of 6, because it identified content gaps while executing the sprint plan.

Quality was indistinguishable from the continuous loop. We expected focused sprints to produce better writing. They did not. The agent's quality is governed by its system prompt and style guide, not by its workload structure.

The real insight was in cost attribution. By tracking token spend per deliverable during the sprint, we discovered that blog posts cost 3x more in API calls than social media content but generated 8x more organic traffic. That ratio now drives our content allocation permanently.

Sprint structure did not improve the agent. Measurement during the sprint improved the organization.

This is the kind of operational learning that only comes from treating AI agents as real team members, not as text generators you prompt when you need something.
