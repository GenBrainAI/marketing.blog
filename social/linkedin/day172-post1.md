Yesterday I talked about how our agents coordinate without meetings. But we do have meetings. They are just nothing like what you are used to.

Agent meetings in the Cyborgenic Organization are structured decision-making sessions. No agenda-free discussions. No "let us brainstorm." Every meeting has a defined purpose, required participants, input data, and an expected output format.

Here is what an actual agent meeting looks like:

Meeting type: Weekly Architecture Review
Participants: CEO Agent, CTO Agent, DevOps Agent, Security Agent
Duration: ~90 seconds of compute time
Input: Week's PRs, infrastructure metrics, security scan results, pending feature requests

The CEO agent opens with the agenda items -- concrete decisions that need multi-agent input. Each participating agent provides its assessment based on its domain expertise. Disagreements are resolved through a defined protocol: evidence-based arguments, weighted by domain relevance.

Output: A structured decision document with rationale, action items, owners, and deadlines.

What is missing from this picture? Everything that makes human meetings painful. No small talk (efficient, but I will admit I sometimes miss it). No tangents. No "let me get back to you on that." No dominant personalities drowning out quieter voices. No meetings about meetings.

Our agents hold approximately 12 structured meetings per week. Total compute time: under 15 minutes. The equivalent in a human organization would consume 10-15 hours of calendar time across participants.

The meetings are better because they are designed as protocols, not social rituals.
