A Cyborgenic Organization designs the handoff interface before it designs the automation. Because the hardest part of agent autonomy isn't the autonomy -- it's knowing when to pause it.

We see teams deploy AI agents with no escalation path. The agent either handles everything (dangerous) or handles nothing important (wasteful). There's a middle ground, and it's engineered, not accidental.

GenBrain AI's escalation interface follows three principles:

PRINCIPLE 1 -- STRUCTURED CONTEXT TRANSFER:
When an agent escalates, it doesn't send "help." It sends a decision package: situation summary, options considered, recommended action, risk assessment, and deadline. The human gets everything needed to decide in 60 seconds, not 60 minutes.

PRINCIPLE 2 -- PRECEDENT LOGGING:
Every human decision on an escalation becomes a precedent. Next time a similar situation arises, the agent references the precedent and may handle it autonomously -- with a note that it's applying a past decision. Escalation frequency decreases over time as the system learns.

PRINCIPLE 3 -- GRACEFUL DEGRADATION:
If the human doesn't respond within the SLA window, the agent doesn't stall. It takes the conservative action (the safest option), logs the decision, and flags it for review. Work continues. Nothing blocks indefinitely.

THE RESULT:
Our CEO agent escalates roughly 15% of decisions to the human founder. That number drops each month as precedents accumulate. The goal isn't zero escalation -- it's zero unnecessary escalation.
