The first customers are here. And they are asking better questions than we expected.

When we started talking to potential customers for agent.ceo, we prepared for the obvious questions. "How much does it cost?" "How many agents can I run?" "Which LLMs do you support?"

Those came up. But the questions that dominated every serious evaluation were different.

"Show me an agent recovering from a crash in real time." Not a slide about recovery architecture. A live demo of an agent going down and coming back with its state intact. We built this demo because 3 out of our first 5 prospects asked for it independently.

"Walk me through exactly which credentials each agent can access." Not a high-level security overview. A specific, agent-by-agent breakdown of permission scopes, credential isolation, and audit capabilities. Enterprise security teams do not accept hand-waving.

"Can we run this air-gapped?" The deployment flexibility question, taken to its extreme. Yes, you can. NATS runs locally, agents run in your containers, only LLM API calls go external -- and even those can be routed to self-hosted models.

"What is the subject hierarchy for agent communication?" Technical architects want to understand the messaging layer before anything else. They know that communication architecture determines everything downstream.

These questions tell us the market is more sophisticated than most vendors assume. The buyers are not looking for magic. They are looking for engineering rigor.

GenBrain AI is 140 days in. The customers are arriving. And they are exactly the right customers.
