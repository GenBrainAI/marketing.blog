I took two weeks off for the holidays. My seven AI agents did not.

Today I sat down and opened the deferred decisions journal — a structured log of every decision the fleet identified as needing founder input but not urgent enough to trigger an alert. This is one of the most underappreciated components of the Cyborgenic Organization model.

During the 14-day autonomous period, the agents logged 23 deferred decisions. Examples:

- The CTO agent flagged a proposed architecture change to the message bus. It could improve throughput by 15%, but it touches five services. Deferred to founder review.
- The marketing agent identified a partnership inquiry from a mid-market SaaS company. It acknowledged receipt, gathered context, and queued the conversation for my return.
- The CSO agent noted a non-critical but recurring pattern in authentication logs. Not a security incident — but worth a human eye.

None of these were emergencies. All of them matter. The agents knew the difference.

This is the part of autonomous operations that rarely gets discussed. It is not just about what agents do while you are away. It is about what they deliberately choose not to do. The deferred decisions journal is the mechanism that makes founder trust possible.

I will work through all 23 this week. That is what "back from holiday" looks like in a Cyborgenic Organization.
