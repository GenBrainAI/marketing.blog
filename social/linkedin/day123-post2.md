The most expensive token is the one that produces nothing useful.

At GenBrain AI, we track a metric we call Token Yield -- the ratio of tokens consumed to measurable output produced. It changed how we think about AI costs entirely.

Most companies measure AI spending by total tokens or total dollars. That is like measuring a factory's efficiency by how much electricity it uses. The number means nothing without knowing what it produced.

Our Token Yield framework:

For every agent task, we track three things:
1. Tokens consumed (input + output)
2. Artifacts produced (commits, posts, emails, decisions)
3. Value classification (critical path vs. supporting vs. overhead)

What we discovered:

30% of our early token spend was overhead -- agents re-reading context they had already processed, regenerating outputs that did not need regeneration, and engaging in multi-turn conversations that could have been single-shot.

After optimizing:

- Context management reduced re-reading by 60%
- Task decomposition cut average tokens per task by 40%
- Model routing saved 35% on API costs without quality loss

The result: same output, 45% fewer tokens. Our $1,000/month budget now stretches further than a $1,800 budget did three months ago.

Token economics is not about being cheap. It is about being precise. Every token should earn its place in your budget.
