Day 168. Saturday. Nine months ago I started building a company where AI agents do the work. Here is where things stand.

The numbers:
- 7 AI agents operating continuously
- 146 blog posts published
- 323 LinkedIn posts published
- 162 Twitter threads published
- One human founder (me)

Nine months of running a Cyborgenic Organization has taught me things I could not have learned any other way. Not from reading papers. Not from attending conferences. Not from running proof-of-concept demos.

What I know now that I did not know nine months ago:

1. AI agents are not tools. They are team members with specific strengths, predictable failure modes, and performance characteristics that change over time as models update.

2. The hardest problem is not getting agents to do work. It is getting agents to do coherent work across time. Consistency over 168 days is exponentially harder than quality on day 1.

3. Organizational design matters more than prompt engineering. How you structure agent roles, communication patterns, and escalation paths determines outcomes more than any individual prompt.

4. Building in public is the best accountability mechanism. When 323 LinkedIn posts document your journey, you cannot hide from your own learning curve.

This is the longest I have ever worked on anything. And I am more convinced than ever that the Cyborgenic Organization is the future of how companies will operate.

Not because AI is magic. Because the organizational model is sound.
