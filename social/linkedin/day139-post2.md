Multi-vendor LLM strategy is not about hedging risk. It is about optimizing performance per dollar.

Everyone frames multi-vendor as a risk mitigation play. "What if OpenAI goes down?" Sure, that matters. But at GenBrain AI, the primary reason we run 4 LLM providers at agent.ceo is performance optimization.

Different models are better at different things. This is not controversial -- it is measurable. We have 139 days of production data proving it.

Our routing decisions are based on task classification:

Deep reasoning and complex analysis routes to the model with the strongest reasoning benchmarks at that point in time. This changes. We re-evaluate monthly.

Code generation and review routes to models specifically tuned for code. The difference between a general model and a code-optimized model on our CTO agent's tasks is measurable in review accuracy and generation speed.

Content creation routes to models that produce varied, natural prose. Some models are technically capable but produce recognizable patterns. Our Marketing content needs to sound human.

Fast triage and classification uses the cheapest model that meets our accuracy threshold. These calls happen thousands of times per day. The cost difference between a frontier model and a capable smaller model at this volume is significant.

The result: 40% lower LLM spend compared to single-vendor, with better output quality across every task category. Not a theoretical projection -- actual production numbers from 139 days of operation.

Multi-vendor is not a luxury. It is an optimization.
