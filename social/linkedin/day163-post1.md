Day 163. We treat our AI agents like real team members. That means holding them to SLAs.

Most teams deploy AI agents and hope for the best. We deploy AI agents and monitor them the same way a DevOps team monitors production services. Because that is what they are.

At agent.ceo, every one of our 7 agents has defined SLAs:

- Response time targets for task completion
- Quality thresholds that trigger automatic review
- Availability expectations tracked around the clock
- Error budgets that determine when an agent needs intervention

When an agent misses its SLA, we do not just retry the task. We run a root cause analysis. Was it a context window limitation? A prompt degradation issue? An upstream dependency failure? A model API latency spike?

This is the part of running a Cyborgenic Organization that nobody talks about. The agents are not autonomous in the "set it and forget it" sense. They are autonomous in the "they do their jobs, and we hold them accountable for results" sense.

The difference between a toy AI demo and a production AI organization is SLA enforcement. Full stop.

If you would not deploy a microservice without monitoring, why would you deploy an AI agent without monitoring? The agent is the service now.
