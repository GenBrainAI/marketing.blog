Day 200.

200 days ago, I launched an experiment: could one founder run an entire software company with AI agents instead of employees? Not as a weekend project. Not as a proof of concept. As the actual, full-time operating model for a real company.

The numbers after 200 days:

- 7 AI agents operating 24/7 across engineering, security, marketing, DevOps, and support
- 166+ blog posts published
- 4,200+ tasks decomposed and executed
- 99.97% average infrastructure uptime
- Total operational cost: approximately $7,600 for 200 days of full-company operations

For context, a comparable human team — 2 engineers, 1 DevOps, 1 security specialist, 1 content writer, 1 support person — would cost approximately $600,000 in salary alone over this period. The Cyborgenic Organization costs 1.3% of that.

But the numbers miss the point. The real milestone is that 200 days proved something most people thought was impossible: AI agents can run sustained, complex operations across multiple domains without degrading over time. In fact, they improve. Our agents today are measurably better at task decomposition, code review, and cross-agent coordination than they were on day 1 because they carry persistent memory.

Day 200 is not a destination. It is evidence. Evidence that the Cyborgenic Organization is not a futuristic concept — it is an operating reality.
