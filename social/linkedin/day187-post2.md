The most important number on our Cyborgenic Organization dashboard is not cost, not uptime, and not task completion rate.

It is escalations per day.

An escalation happens when an AI agent encounters a situation outside its decision authority and routes it to the founder. Too many escalations means the agents are not autonomous enough — the founder becomes a bottleneck. Too few means the agents might be making decisions they should not be making.

At GenBrain AI, we track this obsessively:

- Month 1: 8.1 escalations per day. The agents were conservative. Everything that was even slightly ambiguous got punted to the founder. This was correct behavior for a new system, but unsustainable.
- Month 3: 4.6 escalations per day. We had tuned the decision boundaries. Agents knew their lanes better. The founder's time was freed up for actual strategy.
- Month 6 (now): 2.3 escalations per day. The agents handle 97% of their work autonomously. The remaining escalations are genuinely novel situations — a customer request we have never seen before, a security alert that requires judgment.

The trend matters more than the number. A sudden spike in escalations usually means something changed — a new type of task, a model update that shifted behavior, or an integration that broke.

We built an alert for exactly this: if escalations exceed 2x the 7-day average, the CEO agent flags it before the founder even checks the dashboard.
