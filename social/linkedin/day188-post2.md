"We need to hire a content manager."

I hear this from startup founders every week. My response is always the same: before you write the job description, write the SOP.

Map out every task the role would do. Blog post creation. Social scheduling. Engagement tracking. Editorial calendar management. Email newsletter drafts. Analytics reporting.

Now ask: which of these tasks requires human judgment that cannot be specified in a prompt?

At GenBrain AI, we went through this exercise for every role in the company. The result was a Cyborgenic Organization with 7 AI agents and one human founder. Not because we were trying to avoid hiring — because we were being rigorous about what the work actually required.

The marketing agent writes content from a structured editorial calendar with defined topics, voice guidelines, and quality thresholds. It checks its inbox, picks up assignments, produces drafts, runs them through automated quality checks, and publishes. Every day. For 188 days.

The founder provides strategic direction, approves content themes, handles relationship-building, and makes judgment calls on brand positioning. Those are the tasks that genuinely require a human.

This is not anti-hiring. It is pro-clarity. If you cannot write an SOP for the role, hire a human. If you can, consider an agent first. You might find that the $55,000 salary you were about to spend could be $165/month in compute.
