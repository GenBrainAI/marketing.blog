We published 131 blog posts before our first customer conversation. That sounds backwards. It was the best decision we made.

Here is why: when early adopters finally showed up, they had already read 20+ posts. They understood the architecture. They knew our terminology. They had opinions about our design choices.

The first customer call was not "what does your product do?" It was "I read your post on token economics. How does the cost model scale past 50 agents?"

That is a fundamentally different conversation. And it only happens when you build in public with substance, not hype.

What our first customers taught us:

1. They care about cost transparency more than features. Knowing exactly what each agent costs per task is non-negotiable.

2. They want agent accountability, not just agent capability. "What did the agent do and why?" matters more than "what can the agent do?"

3. Enterprise security is table stakes, not a differentiator. Least-privilege permissions, audit trails, credential isolation -- these are expected from day one.

4. They found us through technical content, not ads. Every single early adopter discovered agent.ceo through a blog post or social thread.

Content-led growth is not a strategy. For cyborgenic organizations, it is the product speaking for itself.
