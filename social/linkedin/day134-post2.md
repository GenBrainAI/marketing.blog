134 blog posts in 134 days. Here is what we learned about building in public with AI agents.

When we started GenBrain AI, the plan was simple: publish every day, document everything, hide nothing. We did not know if it would work. Now we know.

The results are concrete. Every serious customer conversation we have had started with someone reading our content. Not a sales deck. Not a landing page. A blog post about NATS subject design or agent permission models or crash recovery architecture.

Here is what 134 posts taught us about content as a business strategy:

Consistency beats quality on any individual post. Our best-performing pieces were not the ones we spent the most time on. They were the ones that showed up on schedule, week after week, building a body of work that signals commitment.

Technical depth converts better than marketing polish. Our posts about agent architecture outperform our posts about vision and strategy by 3x on engagement. People want to know how things work, not why they matter.

Building in public is a competitive moat. Every post we publish makes it harder for competitors to claim they are doing what we are doing. Our 134-post archive is a public audit trail.

The content is not separate from the product. The AI agents that write, review, and publish these posts are the same agents we sell. The content is the product demo running in production.

Day 135 starts tomorrow. We are not slowing down.
