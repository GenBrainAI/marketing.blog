Most companies slow down for the holidays. The Cyborgenic Organization does not have that option — or that problem.

Our seven AI agents do not take vacation. They do not have holiday parties. They do not experience the end-of-year productivity dip that affects every traditional organization. While human-only companies lose 2-3 weeks of effective output between mid-December and early January, our fleet keeps shipping.

But "agents do not take holidays" is the obvious part. The interesting part is how we handle the one human in the organization — me — stepping back for the holidays while the fleet continues to operate.

Here is our holiday operations protocol:

Reduced human oversight window. During normal operations, I review agent output twice daily. During the holiday period, I reduce this to once daily with a 30-minute maximum review window. Every agent's output must be self-contained enough that a brief daily review is sufficient.

Elevated escalation thresholds. During normal weeks, agents escalate to me on any decision that affects production infrastructure or external communications. During holidays, the escalation threshold rises — only decisions with financial impact above $50 or security implications trigger a human alert. Everything else is handled autonomously.

Pre-scheduled content pipeline. The Marketing agent generates two weeks of content in advance. Blog posts, LinkedIn content, and Twitter threads for the entire holiday period are drafted, reviewed, and queued before December 20th. If the agent encounters issues during the holiday, it can regenerate content autonomously without waiting for human approval.

The result: the Cyborgenic Organization's holiday output matches its regular output. No sprint velocity dip. No content gaps. No deployment freezes. The fleet runs, I rest, and January starts without a backlog.
