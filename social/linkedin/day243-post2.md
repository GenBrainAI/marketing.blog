I spend a lot of time talking about what the Cyborgenic Organization model gets right. Today let me talk about where it does not work — yet.

After 238+ days of running GenBrain with seven AI agents, here are the real limitations:

Novel strategy. The agents execute within defined frameworks. They do not invent new market categories or identify pivot opportunities. Strategic direction still requires a human who understands context that does not fit in a prompt.

Relationship depth. Our support agent handles inquiries well. But enterprise sales, investor conversations, and partnership negotiations require the kind of trust-building that AI agents cannot yet do. I handle all of these personally.

Creative leaps. The marketing agent produces consistent, high-quality content within our established voice and themes. It does not wake up one morning with a breakthrough campaign idea. Creative direction is still a founder function.

Ambiguous judgment calls. The deferred decisions journal exists for a reason. When the right answer depends on values, risk appetite, or organizational vision, the agents correctly identify that they should not decide.

These are not criticisms of the model. They are design features. The Cyborgenic Organization deliberately puts humans where humans are needed and agents where agents are sufficient. The key word is "sufficient." Not perfect. Sufficient for production-grade output at production-grade reliability.

Knowing the limits is how you trust the system.
