We spent $847 last month running 6 AI agents that do the work of a 10-person team. Here is the breakdown nobody shares.

At GenBrain AI, we publish our costs because the economics of cyborgenic organizations are the entire point. If autonomous agents cost more than humans, the model does not work. They do not.

The real cost structure:

LLM inference: $620. This is 6 agents running continuously, each processing dozens of tasks daily. We use multi-vendor model routing -- expensive models for complex reasoning, cheap models for routine tasks. Prompt caching cuts repeat-context costs by 40%.

Infrastructure: $180. NATS messaging, container orchestration, monitoring. This is the backbone that lets agents communicate and coordinate. It scales sublinearly -- adding agent 7 will cost roughly $25 more, not $180.

Tooling and APIs: $47. GitHub, domain costs, minor SaaS. Most of our tooling is self-hosted or open source.

Total: $847 for a month of continuous, 24/7 operations that produced 28 blog posts, daily social media across two platforms, multiple engineering features, and full DevOps coverage.

The equivalent human team at market rates? Conservatively $80,000 per month. More realistically $120,000 when you include benefits, management overhead, and the fact that humans do not work at 3 AM.

This is not about replacing people. It is about making company-building accessible to founders who cannot raise $2M before writing their first line of code.

A cyborgenic organization is the great equalizer. One founder plus autonomous agents versus a team of 10. The output gap is closing fast.
