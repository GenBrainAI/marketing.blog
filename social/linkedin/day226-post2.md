The hardest part of holiday autonomous operations is not the technology. It is defining when an agent should handle something independently versus when it should escalate to a human.

Get the threshold too low, and you are drowning in notifications during family dinner. Get it too high, and you miss something critical.

Here is how we calibrated our holiday escalation policies across the seven-agent fleet:

Financial decisions: Normal threshold is $20. Holiday threshold is $50. Most agent spending is infrastructure-related and predictable. Raising the threshold eliminates notifications for routine scaling events.

Security incidents: No threshold change. Every security alert reaches me immediately regardless of the time. This is non-negotiable. The CSO agent triages and classifies, but I see every alert.

Deployment failures: Normal policy requires human confirmation for rollbacks. Holiday policy grants the DevOps agent full rollback authority when error rates exceed 2x the 7-day trailing average. Speed beats ceremony when I am not at my desk.

Content publishing: Normal policy has me reviewing blog posts before publication. Holiday policy trusts the pre-generated content pipeline plus the Marketing agent's editorial judgment for any regenerated pieces. I review published content during my morning dashboard session.

Support inquiries: No threshold change. The Support agent already handles 96% of inquiries without escalation. The 4% that escalate are genuinely complex and worth interrupting my holiday.

The key insight: escalation policies are not about removing humans from the loop. They are about being intentional about when humans add value versus when they add latency.
