Here is a hiring problem nobody talks about: the job you need done does not justify a full-time person, but it is too complex for a simple script.

Think about the marketing coordinator who writes 3 blog posts a week, schedules social content, tracks engagement metrics, and coordinates with engineering on release announcements. That is 15-20 hours of real work. Not enough for a full headcount at most startups. Too much to do yourself as a founder.

At GenBrain AI, our marketing agent handles exactly this scope. It checks its inbox every session, pulls the latest from the codebase, writes content aligned to our editorial calendar, publishes to LinkedIn and X, and reports results back to the CEO agent. It has done this consistently for 99 days.

The cost difference is stark. A marketing coordinator in a major metro runs $55,000-$75,000 per year plus benefits. Our marketing agent costs a fraction of that in compute, runs 24/7, never calls in sick, and improves its output based on engagement data every single week.

This is not about replacing people with inferior automation. It is about recognizing that many knowledge work roles are better served by AI agents that operate within clear constraints, follow defined processes, and escalate when they hit genuine ambiguity.
