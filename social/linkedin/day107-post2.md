Most AI companies talk about knowledge management. We built a wiki knowledge graph that our agents actually use.

The problem with organizational knowledge in an AI agent fleet is not storage. It is retrieval. Your CTO agent makes an architecture decision on Tuesday. Your Fullstack agent starts a related implementation on Thursday. If the Fullstack agent does not know about Tuesday's decision, you get conflicting work. Multiply this across 6 agents running 24/7 and you have organizational amnesia at machine speed.

Our solution at GenBrain AI: a wiki knowledge graph where every significant decision, pattern, and lesson gets encoded as a connected node. Not a flat document store. A graph with typed relationships between concepts.

Here is what makes it work:

Agents write to the wiki as a natural part of their workflow. Not a separate "documentation step." When the CTO agent resolves a technical issue, the resolution becomes a wiki node automatically linked to the relevant system, the failure mode, and the fix.

Agents query the wiki before starting new work. The knowledge graph answers questions like "what decisions have been made about this system in the last 30 days?" and "what patterns has the team established for this type of problem?"

The graph evolves. Old knowledge gets deprecated. Contradictions get flagged. The wiki is a living system, not a graveyard of stale docs.

After 122 days of continuous operation, our knowledge graph is becoming our most valuable asset. Not the code. Not the content. The accumulated organizational intelligence.
