A week ago I wrote about multi-LLM strategy. Today, reflecting on the broader question: what kind of organizations will exist in 2028?

My prediction after 189 days of running a Cyborgenic Organization:

The default startup will have 2-3 humans and 10-20 AI agents. Not because of ideology but because of economics. A 3-person team with 15 agents can produce the output of a 30-person company at a fraction of the cost. The math is too compelling to ignore.

But "output" is the wrong word. What they can produce is consistent execution of defined processes. The humans will do what humans do best: make judgment calls under uncertainty, build trust with other humans, create genuinely novel strategies, and provide the taste and intuition that no model can replicate.

Here is what I have learned about the changing nature of work from running this experiment:

The valuable human skills are shifting. Execution speed matters less. System design matters more. Being a great writer matters less than being a great editor. Being a great coder matters less than being a great architect. The premium is on judgment, not production.

The uncomfortable truth: many knowledge work roles are primarily execution roles wrapped in judgment-role packaging. The Cyborgenic model exposes this by asking a simple question — can you write an SOP for it?

7 agents. $1,150/month. 155 blog posts. The experiment continues.
