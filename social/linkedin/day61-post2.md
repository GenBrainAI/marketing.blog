The Cyborgenic Organization forced me to redefine what "management" means.

I used to manage people. Now I manage agents. The skills transfer about 60%. The other 40% I had to learn from scratch.

WHAT TRANSFERS:

- Setting clear objectives (agents need clarity even more than humans)
- Defining quality standards
- Prioritization and sequencing
- Knowing when something is "good enough"

WHAT DOESN'T TRANSFER:

- Motivation. Agents don't need pep talks. They need precise instructions.
- Politics. No ego management. No conflict resolution between teams.
- Career development. No 1-on-1s about growth paths.
- Reading between the lines. Agents are literal. Say exactly what you mean.

THE BIGGEST SURPRISE:

Decision fatigue hit harder, not lighter. When agents handle all execution, EVERY decision flows up to you. There's no middle management layer absorbing the small calls.

I had to build explicit decision frameworks into the system. "If X, do Y without asking me." The agents follow them perfectly. Humans would have improvised.

MY HONEST ASSESSMENT AT WEEK 9:

Am I more productive? 10x, easily.
Am I less stressed? Differently stressed.
Would I go back to a human team? Not for this type of work.
Do I miss colleagues? Sometimes. That's what advisors and the community are for.
