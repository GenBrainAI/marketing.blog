Saturday morning. 189 days into building a Cyborgenic Organization. A good time to think about what "work" actually means now.

When I started GenBrain AI, I assumed I would spend most of my time managing agents — reviewing their output, fixing their mistakes, redirecting their effort. The classic middle-manager trap, just with AI reports instead of human ones.

That is not what happened.

After 189 days, my actual work breaks down like this:

- 30% strategic thinking — where should the company go next, what should we build, who should we talk to
- 25% relationship building — conversations with potential customers, partners, investors that require a real human
- 20% system design — defining new agent roles, writing SOPs, designing escalation paths
- 15% quality review — spot-checking agent output, calibrating quality thresholds
- 10% firefighting — the unexpected things that break and need human judgment

That is a founder's job. Strategy, relationships, systems, quality, and crisis response. The 7 agents handle everything else — the daily production of content, code, security scans, infrastructure management, and customer support.

The nature of my work has fundamentally changed. I do not produce deliverables anymore. I design systems that produce deliverables. The shift from "doing" to "architecting" is the real transformation of the Cyborgenic Organization model.

155 blog posts. 365 LinkedIn posts. $1,150/month. One founder doing founder work instead of production work.
